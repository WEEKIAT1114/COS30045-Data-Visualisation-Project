## Proposed method

Use moderated, task-based observation with a short think-aloud briefing. Suggested recruitment: 3–5 adult participants resembling the intended audience, with varied familiarity with road-safety statistics; include a keyboard-only session and a phone session where practical. This number is a practical recommendation, not a stated course minimum. Allow approximately 15–20 minutes each. Conduct a second round after important changes if time permits.

Use the same website version and starting selection for each participant. Record version/date, device, viewport, browser, relevant experience and input mode. Ask only necessary background questions; use participant codes P01, P02, etc., rather than names in analysis notes.

## Consent and briefing

Before starting, explain the study purpose, the activities and expected duration, what notes or recordings will be kept, who can access them, and the intended retention/deletion arrangements. Participation is voluntary; participants may decline or stop without penalty. Obtain explicit recording permission if recording. Avoid asking about personal crash or medical experiences. Store consent/contact information separately from task notes and restrict access to the named research team. Fill in real investigator contact details and follow the course's ethics instructions.

Suggested neutral briefing: “We are evaluating the website, not your ability. Please describe what you are thinking as you work. Some tasks may be difficult; that helps us identify improvements. You may stop at any time.” Do not teach the interface before the task unless your study explicitly evaluates a trained-user scenario.

## Tasks and evaluator answer key

Give participants the **task wording only**, not the answer, interface steps or success criterion.

| Task wording | Evaluator success criterion | What to observe |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What happened to the national number between 2020 and 2021? | Identifies an increase from 37,966 to 39,505; approximately 4.1% | Can the reader find the evidence and describe the measure correctly? |
| Which of car drivers, motorcyclists and pedal cyclists had the largest recorded relative change between 2011 and 2021? What limits that comparison? | Pedal cyclists; recognises policy breaks and that indexed counts do not establish risk | Is the index explanation discoverable and understood? |
| For pedal cyclists in 2021, which age group accounts for the largest count? | 40–64; 3,393 admissions | Can a reader connect a road-user question to the age view? |
| In 2021, what share of hospitalisations among people aged 8–16 involved pedal cyclists? | Approximately 43.7% (1,250 of 2,863) | Is the row-share denominator clear? |
| A friend says the 40–64 age group must face the highest injury risk because its bar is longest. How would you respond? | Explains that counts, unequal age bands, population sizes and travel exposure limit that conclusion | Does the narrative prevent a materially incorrect conclusion? |
| Find the exact national count for 2014, then return to the original view. | 35,515; returns to 2021/all/count modes | Can readers find precise values and recover from selections? |

Optional keyboard task: retrieve a value and change a selection using only the keyboard. Observe focus visibility and whether tooltips/data tables offer equivalent information; do not mistake simply reaching the control for understanding the result.

## Measures

For each task record: unaided success / success with assistance / unsuccessful; response accuracy; elapsed time; errors or wrong turns; assistance given; participant confidence or difficulty rating using a clearly stated scale (for example 1 = very easy, 5 = very difficult); and short verbatim comments with permission. Do not prompt a correct answer. Use neutral probes such as “What are you looking for?”

After tasks, ask: What was unclear? Did a chart or control behave unexpectedly? What did you think the percentages meant? Which information was hardest to find? Include a final opportunity for feedback.

## Analyse, refine and report

Separate observation from interpretation. For example, “P02 reported the percentage as population risk” is an observation; “the denominator label was insufficient” is a possible explanation to investigate. Prioritise failures that lead to incorrect interpretation, inaccessible actions or task abandonment. For each change record the evidence, rationale, before/after screenshot and retest result. Document unresolved issues honestly.
